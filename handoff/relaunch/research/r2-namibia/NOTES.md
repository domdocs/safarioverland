## Why each stay

- **Bagatelle.** Dune chalets on stilts on a red dune, plus a sundowner drive. That makes the first Kalahari night more than a stopover. ExpertAfrica's reviewers are lukewarm on the guiding and do not recommend the captive cheetah experience, so copy must not lead with cheetahs. The rate source is weak: a safari.com search snippet, ZAR 3,800 HB for 1 Mar–31 Oct 2026, which the page itself did not render.
- **Kalahari Anib (alt).** A cheaper option that is tar nearly all the way, and the guided activities are published with prices. It is a Gondwana group lodge, so less individual.
- **Sossus Dune Lodge.** The only lodge inside the Sesriem gate. Guests use the inner gate, which opens about an hour before the main gate. That is the single most useful thing at Sossusvlei, and it is good value on NWR's DBB rate (N$4,030 Nov 2026–Jun 2027). NWR service reputation is something Dom should judge. I have not sourced it.
- **Kulala Desert Lodge (alt).** Wilderness collection, fully inclusive, with a private gate to the dunes on the Tsauchab. US$508 off-peak, US$679 peak, so at the top of our band or over it. I rejected Little Kulala and &Beyond Sossusvlei Desert Lodge on price.
- **Swakopmund hotels.** No Swakopmund property I found meets §4 criterion 3 (in-house guiding). I've typed both as `on-the-road` and made the Living Desert tour the guided element. That operator publishes N$1,000 pp, cash only, rates valid to 31 Oct 2026, so it needs re-checking for 2027. The Delight is the honest-value pick. The Strand is the sea-view upgrade, but it has 125 rooms. Dom may prefer a smaller guesthouse here. I did not research one.
- **Camp Kipwe.** Granite-kopje bungalows near the Twyfelfontein engravings, with an elephant drive and an engravings excursion. 2026 rates come from Chalo Africa (N$5,070/6,700 HB). I could not establish ownership or a conservancy link. The official 2027 rack PDF would not extract.
- **Damaraland Camp (alt).** It has the strongest community-ownership story in Damaraland: a Torra Conservancy partnership, part-owned by the community. It is over budget from April to December (US$697–990). Self-drive rules conflict: park-and-transfer at a C39 farmstead, 4x4 drive-in, or "no self-drive in the conservancy". Wilderness must confirm.
- **Grootberg Lodge.** This is the brief's anchor. It is 100% owned by the ≠Khoadi-//Hôas Conservancy (GSTC report) and managed under contract by Journeys Namibia, and it offers rhino tracking on foot. Self-drivers park at a guarded gate and are driven up unless they are skilled 4x4 drivers. **The rate basis is unclear.** The booking site shows "from NAD 4,520/night" without saying per room or per person. I've recorded US$138 as if per room and flagged it. The rhino tracking price is unpublished.
- **Palmwag Lodge (alt).** Drive-in with fuel on site, and rhino tracking and a sleep-out with published prices (rhino N$4,375 pp). It is a Gondwana lodge. It suits travellers who would rather not hand over the car at a mountain foot.
- **Okaukuejo.** I chose it despite it being below the band and NWR-run, because the floodlit waterhole inside the fence is the best night-time game viewing on the route and staying inside avoids the sunset exit. Recommend Waterhole Chalets (N$2,380 ≈ $145).
- **Etosha Safari Lodge (alt).** A lodge rather than a rest camp, 10 km from Andersson Gate, with guided drives that leave before sunrise. Gondwana.
- **Onguma Bush Camp.** A private reserve at the eastern gate with walks, hides and a waterhole in front of the deck. The official 2025 rack sheet shows DBB basis plus an N$275 conservation levy. Chalo Africa calls the same numbers "all inclusive", which I believe is an error.
- **Namutoni (alt).** The cheap inside-the-park option in the east.
- **Okonjima Plains Camp.** AfriCat and leopard telemetry tracking. Official 2026 rack sheet: N$4,830/5,320 DBB plus an N$350 park levy, with activities extra. **Cheetah work** (named in the brief) no longer appears on Okonjima's activity list. The current list is leopard, rhino, pangolin and brown hyena tracking, plus "Into AfriCat". Copy should say leopard, not cheetah.
- **Okonjima Bush Camp (alt).** Over band (US$619–681 DBB). The cheaper upgrade is a Plains Camp View Room (N$7,000–7,700 ≈ $427–470). Dom may prefer that as the alt instead.

## Rejected, and why

- **Andersson's at Ongava.** Chalo Africa lists NAD 23,400 pppn for 2026 (≈ US$1,430). That is far outside the band. I couldn't open the official sheet to check, but it is clearly not a value pick.
- **Safarihoek (Etosha Heights).** About NAD 8,635–10,395 pppn fully inclusive (≈ $527–635). Self-drive access is unconfirmed (airstrip transfers are mentioned). It is a credible future addition for a west-Etosha variant via Galton Gate.
- **Spitzkoppen Lodge as an overnight.** Ownership sources conflict (one says the community, another a private family). A news report on community anger over its construction on a heritage site turned up in search, but the article now returns 404. I couldn't verify it, so I left the lodge out and kept Spitzkoppe as a day stop with the community rest camp.
- **Mowani Mountain Camp, Doro Nawas.** Both are plausible Twyfelfontein options, but I found no clean 2026 rates. Doro Nawas self-drive access is unconfirmed. They can come back later.
- **Desert Rhino Camp / Hoanib.** Fly-in or remote transfer, and well above band. Possible future "hand over the keys" set piece.
- **Ondekaremba Lodge (airport).** info-namibia lists it as closed.

## Open questions and things Dom must confirm

1. **Windhoek night 1.** I did not pick a stay. A city guesthouse won't meet §4 criterion 3, so this may be the first "on the road" night or a booked-but-unlisted night. Candidates seen: Etango Ranch Guest Farm (about 10 minutes from the airport, ~US$214 per room per an aggregator, undated) and Airport Lodge (rates on request). Alternatively, travellers on early arrivals could drive straight to the Kalahari (260 km of tar). Dom to decide.
2. **Sossusvlei self-drive access.** ATTA (Dec 2025) reported private vehicles restricted to the 2x4 car park from 15 Dec 2025. Viatu (updated Oct 2026) says MEFT confirmed in May 2026 that 4x4 self-drivers may continue. Until a primary source (MEFT/NWR) settles it, the route uses the lodge's guided excursion. Partners in Windhoek should confirm.
3. **Park fees.** N$280 pp/day for international adults at premium parks from 1 April 2026 (safarifind, Observer24, gazette PDF that I could not extract). Viatu still shows N$80 at Sesriem. Confirm at the gate or with MEFT.
4. **Grootberg rate basis** (per room or per person; meal basis) and the rhino tracking price. Ask Journeys Namibia.
5. **Etosha main road.** Upgrade works and detours between Okaukuejo and Halali were reported in 2026. Check the status before publishing day 12 timings.
6. **Drive distances** for days 3, 7, 8, 10 and 14 are estimates assembled from segments, and are marked as such. They need checking against a routing tool or the partners in Windhoek before the road book is printed. Day 7 (Swakopmund to Twyfelfontein via Spitzkoppe and Uis) is the long one. If it runs past 6–7 hours in practice, consider splitting it with a night at Spitzkoppe or Uis, or adding a second Damaraland night (see variants).
7. **Solitaire bakery.** One source says Cafe van der Lee has taken over from Moose McGregor's. Don't name the bakery in copy until checked.
8. **Swakopmund.** Is Dom happy to list town hotels as "on the road", or should we find a smaller guesthouse?
9. **Over-band alternatives:** Damaraland Camp (to ~$990 peak), Kulala (to $679), Okonjima Bush Camp (~$619–681). §4 allows higher prices if we say so. Dom to confirm he wants all three.
10. **Gravel speed limit.** Sources give 80 or 100 km/h as the legal limit. I didn't find a Roads Authority source. Copy says "80 km/h maximum" as advice, not as the law.
11. **Night-driving rule.** I couldn't read actual rental contract wording. Copy should say "many rental agreements restrict it" until a partner confirms.
12. **Images.** No lodge images were gathered (per §5). Every stay needs an image placeholder.
