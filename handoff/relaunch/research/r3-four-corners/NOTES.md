# R3 — The four corners: research notes

**Researcher:** route planner (Claude), 9 November 2026. **Companion file:** `route.json` in this folder.
All rates are indicative. They come from operator or agent pages and are labelled "from". NAD/ZAR conversions use about 16.4 to US$1 (currency.me.uk, data to 5 Nov 2026). The Namibian dollar is pegged to the rand at par, but that peg has no source URL in this research, so treat it as unverified.

## 1. The shape I chose and why

| Days | Leg | Primary | Alternative(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Victoria Falls (no car) | Victoria Falls Safari Lodge (from US$288 B&B) | Ilala Lodge (from US$271 B&B) |
| 3–4 | Vic Falls → Kasane (car collected) → western Chobe | Muchenje Safari Lodge (from US$585 FI) | Chobe Safari Lodge (US$430–600 FI); Chobe Game Lodge (from US$990 FI, splurge) |
| 5–6 | Chobe → Ngoma border → Katima → Kwando | Nambwa Tented Lodge (≈US$732 FI) | Namushasha River Lodge (≈US$222 B&B); Nkasa Lupala (≈US$224 HB) |
| 7–8 | Kwando → Divundu / Popa Falls (optional) | Nunda River Lodge (rate unverified) | Divava Okavango Lodge (≈US$252–308 HB) |
| 9 | Divundu → Ngoma — *on the road* | Chobe River Camp (≈US$159 B&B) | Zambezi Mubala Lodge (≈US$216 B&B) |
| 10 | Ngoma → Kasane (car returned) → Victoria Falls | — | Livingstone variant: Tongabezi (US$735 FI), Waterberry (from US$600) |

The total is 9 nights. Without the panhandle (Nkasa Rupara instead of Divundu) it is 8 nights, and with the Livingstone variant it is 10.

**The main recommendation is not to have a car in Zimbabwe.** Travellers spend the first two nights at the Falls without a vehicle, are transferred to Kasane, and collect the car there. They return it in Kasane at the end. This keeps the vehicle out of Zimbabwe, which removes the Zimbabwe vehicle import paperwork for hire cars. Fuel shortages and roadblocks stop being our problem too. Two more reasons:
- Hire cars collected in Victoria Falls are reported as not normally allowed to cross borders (one guide), and at least one major southern African 4x4 supplier has a Kasane depot but none in Victoria Falls.
- The route already doubles back through Kasane, so a Kasane pickup and drop-off costs nothing in time.

**I return via Ngoma rather than Wenela/Sesheke.** The Zambian M10 between Sesheke and Kazungula (about 135 km) is described as "very poor" and drivers route through Botswana to avoid it. Reconstruction was contracted and funded in 2026, but I could not confirm any progress. If it is rebuilt, a loop that goes out via Ngoma and back via Wenela → Sesheke → Kazungula → Livingstone → Vic Falls Bridge becomes the natural "four corners" circuit. Revisit in 2027.

**Day 9 is long**: about 380–400 km and 5–6 hours from Divundu to Ngoma, all distances unverified. That is the cost of the panhandle. Ways to soften it:
- Use Zambezi Mubala Lodge (40 km east of Katima) instead. This moves roughly an hour onto day 10.
- Take a second night on the Kwando on the way back. That would make the trip 10 nights.

## 2. Border findings (the key question)

Can a rental vehicle legally cross all of them? **Yes, with conditions.** It needs the rental company's written authorisation (a letter of authority) for every country, issued at booking. The company's insurance must also cover each country. Policies differ by company:
- One major 4x4 supplier (Kasane depot): insurance valid in Botswana, Namibia, Zambia and Zimbabwe, with a once-off cross-border surcharge (quoted as ZAR 800 when leaving South Africa). Towing is covered only in southern Zambia (Livingstone District). Outside Botswana and Namibia, third-party cover is bought at the border. Source: https://www.drivesouthafrica.com/?p=133707
- Hertz South Africa does not allow its vehicles into Zimbabwe, Zambia or Angola: https://hertz.co.za/cross-border-policy
- Avis Botswana (Francistown) does not allow its vehicles into Zambia (search summary of https://www.avis.com/amp/es/locations/bw/francistown/frw; I did not open the page).
- **Zimbabwe and hire cars:** Zimbabwean press (2017) reports that hired foreign vehicles need a *Commercial* Temporary Import Permit with a commercial vehicle guarantee, not the ordinary TIP. I could not open those articles, so I am working from search summaries. This needs ZIMRA confirmation. It is the strongest reason for the Kasane pickup.

The PDF of the Botswana list is hosted on a third-party site (pcf11.org). The header reads "Department of Immigration and Citizenship, Government of Botswana, updated April 2025". It would be better to find a gov.bw copy.

**Vehicle charges at the border.** All of these are dated or conflicting, so treat every figure as unverified until checked:
- **Zimbabwe:** road access fee US$10 per entry, third-party insurance US$30 per month, carbon tax US$10 per month. This is ZIMRA's own sheet, but its metadata dates it to 2018. Documents: registration book, police clearance, owner's letter of authority.
- **Botswana:** road permit BWP 50–65, third-party insurance about BWP 50 (90 days), road safety levy BWP 50 (a year). Sources range from 2023 to undated.
- **Namibia:** cross-border charge N$350 per car (Namibia Wetlands page, undated), or NAD 300 (Avis). A Road Fund Administration increase has been reported.
- **Zambia:** carbon tax by engine size (paid in kwacha), road access fee or tolls, third-party insurance or COMESA yellow card, council levy. No reliable current amounts found.

**KAZA UNIVISA:** it covers Zimbabwe and Zambia only. It costs US$50, lasts 30 days, and allows day trips into Botswana via Kazungula (UK FCDO, both country pages). An overnight stay in Botswana ends it. **It does not suit this route**, because travellers sleep in Botswana and Namibia. Expansion to Botswana and Namibia was agreed in principle in 2024. One 2025 article claims five countries are already covered, but nothing else I found supports it. A separate SADC UniVisa pilot (Angola, Mozambique, Namibia, South Africa, Zimbabwe) is still in technical preparation (2026). **The status of any expansion is unverified because sources conflict.** The practical effect: travellers leave Zimbabwe on day 3 and re-enter on day 10, so they need a double-entry Zimbabwe e-visa or two single entries. Namibia has required visas for US and UK citizens since 1 April 2025 (on arrival or e-visa). Zambia and Botswana are visa-free for both.

## 3. Rejected or parked options

- **Ngoma Safari Lodge** (Chobe Enclave): from US$768 off-season up to US$1,365 fully inclusive May–Oct 2026, and closed January to March 2026 for a refurbishment. Ownership has changed (Machaba Safaris reported). Worth looking at once rates settle, because it is community-linked.
- **The Elephant Camp, Matetsi, Victoria Falls River Lodge** (existing listings): at US$835–1,087 or more, The Elephant Camp is over the band for a first night with no car. They suit R1 better. They could be offered as an upgrade.
- **The Old House, Kasane**: a small riverside guesthouse that suits self-drivers, but I found no rates and reviews are mixed. Possible budget alternative later.
- **Kazile Island Lodge, Camp Kwando, Jackalberry, Serondela**: not researched, out of time. Jackalberry and Serondela belong to Wild Waters Lodges, the same group as Nkasa Lupala.
- **Shakawe / Botswana panhandle stays** (Drotsky's, Xaro): not researched. Kept only as a variant.
- **Protea Hotel Katima / Caprivi River Lodge**: workable overnight stops, but no rates found and they fail "only it offers". Chobe River Camp and Zambezi Mubala are better on-the-road picks.

## 5. Open questions / decisions for Dom

1. **Vehicle hub:** do you accept the recommendation to start without a car in Victoria Falls and collect it in Kasane? This affects how our partners in Victoria Falls are used, and assumes a Kasane-based partner (STRATEGY lists "Maun/Kasane to follow").
2. **Price band on the Chobe leg:** every lodge on the Chobe riverfront I found is US$430–1,570 fully inclusive. Muchenje sits at the top of the band, and Chobe Game Lodge is well above it. Do we list Chobe Game Lodge as a declared splurge, or drop it?
3. **Panhandle in or out by default?** It adds 2 nights and creates the long day 9. My view: offer it as the 10-night version, and make Nkasa Rupara the default for the 8-night version.
4. **Livingstone variant:** worth including if it means extra paperwork (Zambia vehicle charges), or only as a transfer-based add-on after the car is returned?
5. **Is Chobe Safari Lodge acceptable?** It is large (107 rooms) and fails "only it offers" on its own. I kept it as the practical in-town option with its own boats.
6. **Ilala vs Victoria Falls Safari Lodge as primary** is a matter of taste. Both overlap with R1, so coordinate with that researcher.
7. **Distances:** several legs have no published figure (Ngoma–Katima, Kongola–Divundu, Divundu–Ngoma). They need checking against a road atlas or a test drive by our partners, then a short drive-time table for the road book.
8. **ZIMRA:** confirm the hire-car rule (Commercial TIP plus guarantee) before any copy mentions driving a rental into Zimbabwe.
