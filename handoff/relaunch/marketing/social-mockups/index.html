Windhoek, the Kalahari, the dunes at Sossusvlei, the cold coast at Swakopmund, Damaraland, and Etosha, then back to Windhoek. Fourteen to sixteen nights, much of it on gravel, and Africa's best-known self-drive country.

Post 4 · Week 1 ThuThe stays
N
Niels van de MeerEditor, Safari Overland · Self-drive journeys in southern Africa1d · Edited

Search for a self-drive safari and most of what comes back is camping.

Rooftop tents. Fully equipped 4×4s with a fridge and a gas bottle. Campsite bookings. For a certain kind of traveller, that is exactly right, and southern Africa does it better than anywhere.

But I keep meeting a different traveller. They want the freedom of their own vehicle: to stop when they like, to take the long way, to sit at a waterhole until they're ready to leave. And they want to finish the day somewhere with a bed, a shower, a fire, and a guide who'll take them out at first light.

That's the trip we plan. The places in the collection aren't all expensive. Many sit between US$150 and US$600 per person per night. What they share is that each one offers something only it offers: a hide sunk at the edge of a pan, a walking guide who grew up in the area, rhino on foot, a community that owns the land.

You drive yourself to the gate. Then someone who knows the place takes over.

— Niels

Post 9 · Week 2 ThuPartners
N
Niels van de MeerEditor, Safari Overland · Self-drive journeys in southern Africa2d

We won't sell a route unless there's someone on the ground at the start of it.

On paper, a self-drive needs a hire car, a route and a list of bookings. In practice, it needs a person who can hand over the vehicle and walk round it with you, who knows which garage in town fixes what, and who answers when a tyre goes on a Sunday afternoon two hours from anywhere.

In Victoria Falls we have that: partners on the ground who meet travellers, hand over the vehicle, and are the backup if something goes wrong.

For Namibia, we're working on the same arrangement in Windhoek. Until it's in place, I'll say so plainly to anyone who asks.

It's the part of the trip nobody photographs. It's also the part that lets you relax on the road.

— Niels

Post 12 · Week 3 TueHonest second opinion
N
Niels van de MeerEditor, Safari Overland · Self-drive journeys in southern Africa3d

Self-drive isn't for everyone, and it's worth saying who it isn't for.

It isn't for you if you'd rather not make a decision for a fortnight. On the road you choose when to leave, where to stop, how long to sit at a waterhole. Some people find that freedom is the whole point. Others find it tiring.

It isn't for you if a long day at the wheel spoils the evening. Some days on our routes are short. A few are long, on gravel, and we say so in the road book.

And it isn't for you if your main goal is seeing as much game as possible in as few days as possible. A fly-in safari does that better.

It is for you if you want the country between the parks as much as the parks themselves.

Most of the people who send us a brief already know which they are. When they don't, we'd rather tell them than sell them the wrong trip.

— Niels
