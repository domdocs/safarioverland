Four countries meet within a few kilometres of each other where the Chobe runs into the Zambezi. From Victoria Falls you can be in Botswana before lunch and in Namibia's Zambezi Region by the afternoon, and a land border is part of the day rather than an interruption to it: a queue under a fig tree, a stamp, a man selling cold drinks from a cool box, the barrier lifting onto a new road.

Done in a rental car, a crossing has two halves. You, with your passport, have to be allowed in. The car, with its papers, has to be allowed out of one country and into the next. Most delays come from the second half. This note covers both, for the countries on our routes.

## Before you leave: the rental company's permission

A rental car cannot cross a border without written permission from the company that owns it. This is usually called a cross-border letter or letter of authority, and it travels with the vehicle's registration papers. Without it, you will be turned back.

- **Ask when you book, not at the desk.** The letter names the countries you plan to enter. Some fleets will not authorise Zimbabwe or Zambia at all, and those that do may need notice to prepare the papers.
- **Expect a fee.** Rental companies usually charge a one-off cross-border fee for the trip. It is separate from the government charges you pay at the border.
- **Check the papers at the handover.** Letter, registration document (or a certified copy, as the company specifies), the car's insurance details, and the country sticker on the back. Put them in one folder and keep it in the car.

When we book the vehicle, we book it for the route, borders included. That means the letter is arranged before you land.

## Your passport and visas

Requirements change, so check the official advice for your passport before you travel. As of late 2026, for US and UK passport holders:

- **Zimbabwe.** A visa is needed, and most visitors get it on arrival or apply online in advance. Single-, double- and multiple-entry visas are available, at different prices. Pay in US dollars, in small notes.
- **Zambia.** No visa needed for UK or US passport holders for tourist visits, since 2022. You may be asked for proof of onward travel and funds.
- **Botswana.** No visa needed for UK or US passport holders for tourist visits.
- **Namibia.** Since 1 April 2025, UK and US passport holders need a visa. You can get it on arrival or apply online in advance; the Namibian authorities recommend applying online to avoid queues. The fee is N$1,600 for travellers over 11.

Make sure your passport has plenty of blank pages. Each crossing uses some, and a full passport is not a problem you can solve at a border post.

## The KAZA UniVisa

The KAZA UniVisa is a single visa for Zimbabwe and Zambia. It is currently being issued: the UK government lists it at US$50, valid for 30 days, covering travel between the two countries and day trips into Botswana through Kazungula. It is sold at a limited number of airports and land borders, including Victoria Falls airport, the Victoria Falls bridge and Kazungula, and can be applied for online.

Whether it is worth having depends on your route, and for many of our travellers it is not:

- Because Zambia is visa-free for UK and US passports, the UniVisa only helps if you cross between Zimbabwe and Zambia more than once, for instance staying at the Falls and spending a day on the Zambian side.
- It does not cover Namibia. Day trips into Botswana are covered, but an overnight in Botswana is reported to cancel it. On a route that sleeps in Kasane or the Zambezi Region and returns to Zimbabwe, a double-entry Zimbabwe visa is the simpler choice.

The UniVisa has been suspended and revived before. Check its status close to travel.

## At the border: what the car pays

At each post you deal with immigration (passports), customs (the car), and sometimes the police and a veterinary checkpoint. Expect to pay some combination of the following for a foreign-registered vehicle, in cash and sometimes in a specific currency:

- **Zimbabwe.** A temporary import permit for the car, third-party insurance, and a carbon tax. Zimbabwe abolished its separate road access fee in July 2026; tolls on the main roads still apply, payable at toll gates.
- **Botswana.** A road permit, a road fund levy and third-party insurance. No carbon tax.
- **Namibia.** A cross-border charge for foreign-registered vehicles entering the country. A car rented in Namibia does not pay this on its way back in.
- **Zambia.** A carbon emission surcharge, paid in kwacha, along with road tolls, a council levy and third-party insurance.

Amounts change, sometimes more than once a year, and we do not print them here for that reason. The road book lists the charges at each post on your route as we last confirmed them, with the currency each one is paid in. Carry US dollars in small notes, some rand or Namibian dollars, and a little pula and kwacha if your route needs them, and keep every receipt until you have left the country.

Veterinary checkpoints, such as at Ngoma on the Botswana–Namibia border, may stop fresh meat and dairy crossing from one zone to another. Shop after the border, not before.

## How long it takes

A crossing is two posts, one on each side, and each has its own queue. On a quiet morning at a small post, the whole thing can take well under an hour. With a bus ahead of you, a line of trucks, or a holiday weekend, it can take much longer. We plan for two hours per crossing in the road book and are pleased when we do not need them.

Opening hours vary by post and change from time to time. The Kazungula bridge post between Botswana and Zambia moved to 24-hour operation in 2026; most of the smaller posts on our routes open early in the morning and close in the evening. Never plan to arrive near closing time. If you miss it, you sleep on the wrong side.

## What we'd do

Keep crossings to the morning, and keep the drive after them short. Arrange the rental company's letter at booking, choose visas by route rather than by habit (a double-entry Zimbabwe visa for most Falls–Chobe–Falls trips, no UniVisa unless Zambia features twice), and apply online where the country allows it.

Put passports, the car's papers, the rental letter, the insurance and a stack of small notes in one folder that stays in the front of the car. Be patient, be polite, and answer the question asked.

And if something at the post doesn't match what you were told, call the line before you argue. Around Victoria Falls, our partners on the ground cross these posts often, and can usually tell you what has changed since the road book was printed.
